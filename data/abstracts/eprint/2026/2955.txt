Bellare et al. (CRYPTO 2022) define a hierarchy of unforgeability notions for non-interactive threshold signatures, TS-UF-0 to TS-UF-4 and TS-SUF-2 to TS-SUF-4. They prove that each notion implies the weaker ones and that TS-UF-0 implies TS-UF-1 with a loss of $\binom{ns}{t-1}$ for $ns$ servers and threshold $t$, and they separate every other pair of notions. Cremers, Peltonen and Zhao (2024) showed that TS-UF-4 does not imply TS-UF-3, and Bellare, Tessaro and Zhu revised the definitions in response. We give machine-checked proofs in EasyCrypt of the revised hierarchy, of the loose implication from TS-UF-0 to TS-UF-1, and of the attacks behind the separations. We also prove that the loss of $\binom{ns}{t-1}$ is optimal for an implication that holds for every scheme, up to the security of a signature scheme.
