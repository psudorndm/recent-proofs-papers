Incrementally verifiable computation ($\IVC$) allows a prover to maintain a short proof that can be efficiently updated after each computation step, without retaining or recomputing the preceding execution. In the context of AI safety, the need for incremental verification is particularly important, as AI systems interact with users over time and perform further computation after each interaction. However, standard $\IVC$ schemes do not directly address a crucial feature of AI tasks: their dependence on external information and expertise. Indeed, the UK AISI Alignment Team~\cite{IrvingM25} emphasizes that verification methods for AI safety should accommodate access to external information and human judgment. This is the topic of this paper. We construct $\IVC$ schemes for {\em oracle-aided} computations under the Learning with Errors ($\LWE$) assumption by first constructing succinct non-interactive arguments ($\SNARG$s), and then extending them to the incremental setting. We consider the following two settings, where below, $\lambda$ is the security parameter, $n$ is the input length, and $T$, $S$, and $Q$ are the time, space, and oracle-query bounds. \begin{itemize} \item \textbf{Robust computations.} For bounded-space computations, including non-deterministic computations, in which every accepting purported execution on a false input contains at least an $\varepsilon$-fraction of incorrect oracle answers, we construct {\em privately verifiable} $\SNARG$s and $\IVC$. For $\IVC$, the corresponding robustness condition is imposed on the claimed configuration. Public parameters, private verification keys, and proofs have length $\poly(\lambda,S,\log T,1/\varepsilon)$, and verification uses $O(\lambda/\varepsilon)$ oracle calls. We complement this result with a lower bound ruling out (deterministic) publicly verifiable $\SNARG$s and $\IVC$s with public parameters of length $o(n)$, and $o(Q)$ verifier oracle queries. \item \textbf{Signed oracles.} For oracles that digitally sign their query-answer pairs, we construct publicly verifiable $\SNARG$s and $\IVC$ without a robustness assumption, and with oracle-free setup and verification. Public parameters and proofs have length $\poly(\lambda,S,\log T)$. For deterministic computations with oracle query and answer lengths $\poly(\lambda,\log T)$, we remove the dependence on $S$, obtaining public parameter and proof lengths $\poly(\lambda,\log T)$. \end{itemize} All our constructions have adaptive completeness and soundness. Sub-exponential hardness of $\LWE$ suffices for all constructions; polynomial hardness suffices in the deterministic signed-oracle setting when $T$ is polynomially bounded in $\lambda$. All cryptographic assumptions are required to hold even given access to the underlying oracle.
