Lookup arguments with sublinear prover time typically rely on bilinear pairings. The only exception is the recent work of Campanelli, Carrillo, Cascudo, Fiore, Francati, and Gennaro (ePrint 2025/238); however, they rely on general-purpose SNARKs to certify expensive preprocessing, which requires non-black-box usage of cryptographic primitives. Thus practical realizations of sublinear lookups with post-quantum security remain challenging. We develop the first plausibly post-quantum sublinear lookup arguments that avoid general-purpose SNARKs via polynomial preprocessing and a novel adaptation of proximity testing for Reed-Muller codes into the Interactive Oracle Proof (IOP) framework. Our starting point is a knowledge-sound polynomial commitment scheme (PCS) with prover-side preprocessing based on the Kedlaya-Umans framework. Rather than proving the expensive preprocessing computation inside a general-purpose SNARK, we directly certify its output using Reed-Muller proximity testing and an associated evaluation protocol. For polynomials of size $N$, our approach yields multivariate and univariate PCS with $N^{1+\epsilon}\mathsf{polylog}(N)$ preprocessing and only $\mathsf{polylog}(N)$ online prover time, while retaining $\mathsf{polylog}(N)$ proof size and verification. We leverage this PCS to obtain a lookup argument for \textit{static} tables of size $N$ and $k\ll N$ lookups with $k\cdot\mathsf{polylog}(N)$ online prover time and $\mathsf{polylog}(N)$ verification and proof size. We further extend the approach to \textit{updatable} tables, obtaining $O(k(N^\epsilon+\mathsf{polylog}(N)))$ amortized prover time for a $k$-sized batch of reads and writes, where $\epsilon \in (0,1)$. We retain $\mathsf{polylog}(N)$ proof size and verification. The IOPs underlying our lookup arguments satisfy round-by-round (knowledge) soundness, which can be compiled into plausibly post-quantum arguments in the (quantum) random oracle model with transparent setup.
