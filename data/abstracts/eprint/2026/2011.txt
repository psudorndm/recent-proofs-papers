Folding lets a prover certify a computation incrementally by combining committed instances into an accumulator. In lattice-based folding, a linear combination of field values can change their canonical digit representations. Re-encoding the result gives the prover a short opening. The verifier must establish that this opening represents the prescribed fold of the original committed values. We present Sonata, a high-arity lattice folding scheme for committed quadratic relations. A quadratic sumcheck certifies canonical encoding and the folded equations together. A recursive short-opening argument binds its final evaluation to the original commitments. We prove knowledge soundness for classical rewindable provers by comparing two labelled extraction contexts and bounding the probability that both yield the same invalid candidate. We evaluate complete five-layer transitions for batches of 16 to 1,024 fresh inputs. Across this range, communication increases from 143,165 to 170,361 bytes when the input commitments and setup are shared. At 1,024 inputs, the median prover and verifier times are 258.3 and 226.2 seconds.
