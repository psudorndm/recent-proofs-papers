Consensus protocols must compress tens of thousands of signatures per block, yet ML-DSA (FIPS 204) signatures are kilobytes long and lack native aggregation. Aerie aggregates Falcon signatures into a single succinct proof per batch without recursion, using a sum-check-based polynomial interactive oracle proof (PIOP) over the Akita lattice-based polynomial commitment. Extending this approach to ML-DSA is challenging: the hash that its verifier recomputes from the response and hint takes seven Keccak-f permutations, or nine for ML-DSA-87, which are expensive over prime fields. We address this bottleneck by proving ML-DSA's algebraic and Boolean components over separate fields, supporting all three FIPS 204 parameter sets. A PIOP over a 64-bit prime field handles the lattice relation, hint verification, and range checks, while Flock proves Keccak over GF(2). We introduce a cross-field parity bridge to enforce bitwise consistency between the two commitments. Using seeded sparse matrices, the bridge detects any fixed nonzero difference except with probability below 2^-144.6, independently of batch size. The aggregate certifies that every signature passes FIPS 204 verification. In the classical random-oracle model, each Fiat-Shamir challenge incurs at most 2^-128 soundness error per query, as in Aerie, and aggregate unforgeability reduces to the strong unforgeability of ML-DSA. On 16 AMD Zen 5 cores with AVX2, our prover aggregates 32,768 ML-DSA-44 signatures in 2.68 s, at 49.2 bytes per signature. For ML-DSA-87, it takes 4.13 s at 82.7 bytes per signature. Against the Johnson-regime variant of Alloul26, a prime-field ML-DSA-87 aggregator, our prover is 43x faster and our aggregates are 1.8x smaller.
