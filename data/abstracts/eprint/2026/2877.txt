BitVM-style protocols verify succinct proofs on Bitcoin by garbling a proof veriﬁer oﬀ-chain. What reaches the chain in a dispute is the proof itself: its bits under one-time keys, the scripts that authenticate them and the signatures that bind them to transactions. Existing designs garble elliptic-curve succinct non-interactive argument of knowledge (SNARK) veriﬁers, which a quantum adversary breaks. Our goal is a post-quantum alternative: a hash-based scalable transparent argument of knowledge (STARK) veriﬁer, garbled and fed with hash-based one-time keys, with no number-theoretic assumption anywhere in the veriﬁer; its price is a far larger proof, and this paper measures and reduces that on-chain data. We compile the complete veriﬁer for WHIR-based STARK proofs from Ziren into a Boolean circuit using free-XOR gadgets for binary-tower arithmetic and Blake3. Our implementation uses Ziren’s binary-ﬁeld proof stage and narrow recursion. For an Ethereum block proved through four recursion levels, the ﬁnal recursive WHIR-proof veriﬁer has 175.4M non-free gates and 520,832 input bits, corresponding to 2.81 GB of garbled tables, and publishing that input with translated Winternitz keys takes 3.93 million virtual bytes (MvB), in 41 standard transactions. The costs are coupled: on-chain cost grows linearly with the input bits, oﬀ-chain storage with the gate count, and proving time exponentially with the grinding that shortens the input. The WHIR schedule, the Merkle cap depth and the input encoding trade these costs against one another, and a model of proof size and proving time selects the setting, a step toward practical post-quantum Bitcoin bridges.
