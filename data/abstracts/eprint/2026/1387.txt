Modern hardware security often relies on the assumption that protections introduced before synthesis will survive logic synthesis. However, Electronic Design Automation (EDA) toolchains apply structural optimizations that can affect data-dependent switching activity. Conventional netlist auditing may also require an IP vendor to disclose proprietary gate-level intellectual property to an external auditor. In this paper, we introduce ZK-Audit, a pre-silicon framework for assessing input-dependent switching in synthesized soft IP. By translating gate-level netlists into a custom Side-Channel Intermediate Language (SCIL), ZK-Audit expresses stable-state transitions and their capacitance-weighted switching scores as arithmetic constraints. In our evaluation, the AES S-box exceeded the configured switching-discrepancy threshold in 86 of 100 trials, illustrating the framework's ability to identify input-dependent switching in a cryptographic component. The assessment applies to the sampled inputs and the audited design, with transient glitches and physical implementation effects outside its scope. We also specify a zero-knowledge Bounded Toggle Assessment (ZK-BTA) relation that would allow a client to verify the reported exceedance count while the vendor keeps its netlist and execution traces private.
