Zero-knowledge proofs for machine-learning inference are often limited by dense arithmetization: even after model pruning, R1CS generators may instantiate witness variables and linear-combination terms according to the original tensor shape. Sparse-zkVC extends zkVC's CRPC+PSQ matrix-multiplication generator with mask-guided synthesis. Given a public static pruning mask, it allocates variables only for retained weights and active prefix-sum states while advancing CRPC coefficients over the original dense index order. This preserves coefficient alignment, keeps the constraint count at the inner dimension $d$, and reduces the dominant variable term from $h d m$ to $h\cdot NNZ$, where $NNZ$ counts retained weights. The prototype removes unused variables and linear-combination terms during frontend synthesis. On the zkVC-aligned $128\times64\times49$ benchmark, 99\% sparsity reduces variables from 412,736 to 12,191 and improves online proving time by 20.2$\times$, with all generated proofs verifying successfully. Long-context and LLaMA-7B linear-operator stress tests characterize the resulting efficiency gains and remaining single-node memory limits.
