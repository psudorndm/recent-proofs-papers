In 2011 we observed that a single path in the Keccak sponge from a state whose capacity has all zero bits back to such a state yields an explicit, infinite family of second preimages for every message of at least one full rate block. Finding such a path costs about 2^(c/2), which lies "beyond the event horizon" for SHA3-256 (c=512). In this paper we give four novel theorems around that observation. First, the capacity can have any value x: a two-block meet-in-the-middle loop from x back to x, found at heuristic cost about 2*2^(c/2), gives the same unlimited family of second preimages requiring no further search, with connectors to and from x at about the same cost again (Theorem 1). Theorem 2 extends this to k-block loops and to cycles of the capacity map. A single-state bridge, a state S with cap(f(S)) = cap(f^-1(S)), where cap denotes the capacity part of the state, also gives such a family but costs about 2^c. Theorem 3 gives cheap collisions and a bridge-amplified multicollision: one inner collision and one connector yield equal-length Joux-style 2^k-multicollisions for every k, instead of Joux's k collision searches. Building on paths to a given inner state, Theorem 4 uses k bridges on the same capacity value x to give k^L full-state multicollisions of 2L+1 blocks after a prefix to x, for every L, at a fixed heuristic cost of E[T_k] ~ Gamma(k+1/2)/Gamma(k)*sqrt(2*2^c/q) with q=2p(1-p), about sqrt(4k)*2^(c/2) for balanced search (p=1/2), that does not depend on L; a binary Joux construction producing at least as many messages needs about ceil(L*log2(k)) collision searches, each of generic order 2^(c/2). Fixed-cost multicollisions were known for CubeHash, but they use the all-zero fixed state and their messages differ in length; to our knowledge, the k^L count of equal-length messages from k>1 bridges at an arbitrary x is new. It has no impact on SHA3-256. We give verified full-round examples on Keccak-f[200] and on Keccak-f[1600] with c=32, a full-round Keccak-f[1600] bridge with c=80, and a full-round c=80 example from the standard IV: an IV transition and three bridges (2^42.61 calls in total) give 3^3=27 distinct 1710-byte messages with a common digest. The second part of the paper offers novel protocols built on the asymmetry between minting and forging Keccak bridges. They include proof of work with hardness-adaptive capacity, dual-capacity commitments anchored by the real SHA3-256 digest, bridges as zero-knowledge-committed tokens, and a self-enforcing bounty market for reduced-round security. To our knowledge, these are the first unkeyed Keccak-based protocols that deliberately set the capacity so that minting a generic bridge takes a feasible O(2^(c/2)) permutation calls, while the tunable hardness parameter c makes forging a bridge infeasible, since it requires O(2^c) permutation calls. We prove the four theorems, machine-check the stated algebraic results in Lean 4, and provide reproducible experiments and open-source proof-of-concept code. The applications are design proposals whose end-to-end security remains open.
