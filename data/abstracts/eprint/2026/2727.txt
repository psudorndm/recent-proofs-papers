Information-theoretic MPC among $n$ parties with $t$ corruptions can securely evaluate a circuit $C$ with $O(|C| \cdot t/(n-t))$ online communication in the preprocessing model and $O(|C| \cdot t/(n-2t))$ communication in the honest majority setting. Damg{\aa}rd, Larsen and Nielsen (CRYPTO 2019) showed that both communication bounds are tight for inner products. Fully homomorphic encryption and other assumptions known to imply public-key cryptography beat both communication bounds, but the communication complexity with symmetric-key cryptography alone is less understood. For MPC with preprocessing, we prove that the same $\Omega(|C| \cdot t/(n-t))$ lower bound holds in the random oracle model, so a random oracle cannot reduce the online communication. For honest majority MPC relative to a random oracle, we present an $\Omega(|C|)$ communication lower bound that is matched by a new construction for degree-2 polynomials (including inner products) when $n = O(\log \lambda)$, beating the information-theoretic bound. We also study quantum MPC. First, we prove that information-theoretic protocols with quantum communication (and preprocessing) cannot reduce communication compared to their classical counterparts. Furthermore, we extend our lower bound for MPC with preprocessing to protocols with a quantum-accessible random oracle, but use only classical communication. In contrast, with both quantum communication and a post-quantum one-way function, we present a simple two-party protocol that evaluates the two-party instance of these circuits with $\mathsf{poly}(\lambda, \log |C|)$ communication, beating these lower bounds. Finally, such communication savings do not come for free. We prove that there exist circuits $C$ such that every blackbox protocol with $o(|C|)$ quantum communication must perform $\Omega(|C|)$ cryptographic operations.
