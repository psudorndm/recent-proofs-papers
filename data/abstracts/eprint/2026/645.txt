Anamorphic encryption (AE) enables covert communication in adversarial settings where the receiver’s secret key may be exposed to a coercive adversary. Although existing AE constructions demonstrate the feasibility of this paradigm, their covert bandwidth rates are severely limited, particularly when built from conventional public-key encryption (PKE) schemes. This work develops a systematic approach to constructing bandwidth-efficient anamorphic extensions of residuosity-based PKEs. We first present a robust bit-wise anamorphic extension of the Goldwasser-Micali cryptosystem and a block variant that substantially improves covert throughput. We then construct an anamorphic extension of the Benaloh cryptosystem using an explicit, uniquely decodable embedding of covert messages together with a redundancy tag for robustness. Beyond individual constructions, we introduce a general Δ-structured public-key encryption framework that captures the common algebraic structure enabling anamorphic extensions and instantiate it with block Goldwasser-Micali, Paillier and Damgård-Jurik encryption. We further provide a formal analysis of key deniability, robustness, message loss and windowed decryption, counter-reuse attacks and forward security against subsequent sender coercion. The security of our constructions is established through IND-NA reductions to pseudorandom-function security. At the 128-bit security level, our Benaloh, block Goldwasser-Micali and Paillier constructions achieve approximately 0.46-0.48 covert bits per ciphertext bit under the comparison metric considered, demonstrating a substantial bandwidth improvement over the representative prior approaches. Overall, the proposed framework provides a unified foundation for designing efficient anamorphic extensions of structured public-key encryption schemes.
