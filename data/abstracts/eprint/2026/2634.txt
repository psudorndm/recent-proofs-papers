The closest vector problem(CVP)and the nearest codeword problem (NCP) are fundamental computational problems with connections to learning, coding theory, and cryptography. We study hardness of approximation in the preprocessing model: an algorithm receives the lattice or code in advance, may perform unrestricted computation to produce polynomial-size advice, and must then solve the problem on any target in polynomial time. Unless $\mathsf{NP} \subseteq \mathsf{P/poly}$, no such algorithm achieves an approximation factor of $\bullet$ $M^{1−\varepsilon}$ for NCP over any fixed finite field, or $\bullet$ $M^{1/\min\{p,2\}−\varepsilon}$ for CVP in any $\ell_p$ norm, for every fixed $\varepsilon > 0$, where $M$ denotes the code block length or lattice dimension, respectively. We improve the subpolynomial factors of Khot, Popat, and Vishnoi [KPV14]to polynomial ones, matching the exponents that Song [Son26] proved without preprocessing for binary codes and the $\ell_2$ norm. We further extend Song’s evaluation tables to every fixed prime characteristic and the lattice reduction to $\ell_p$ with $p > 2$. Our main technical contribution is an extension of the moment method, introduced in an OpenAI report [Ope26] and improved by Song [Son26], to the preprocessing setting. In addition, we give a direct reduction from probabilistically checkable proofs (PCPs) whose query locations and families of possible predicates do not depend on the formula. The resulting factor of $M^{\Omega(1/(\log \log M)^2)}$ is already larger than that of [KPV14], and the reduction may be of independent interest.
