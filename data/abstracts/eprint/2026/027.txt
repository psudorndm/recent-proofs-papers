Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) enables computations to be performed directly on encrypted data, without ever requiring decryption. This capability is particularly crucial for privacy-preserving outsourced computation in sensitive fields such as healthcare and finance. While FHE ensures data confidentiality under the honest-but-curious adversarial model, achieving full malicious security, encompassing both integrity and privacy, requires an additional layer of verifiability. To address this, a growing body of research has explored combining FHE with techniques from verifiable computation, leading to the notion of verifiable FHE (vFHE). However, the integration of these two paradigms often results in substantial computational overhead, making existing approaches largely impractical for real-world deployment. Although there are many approaches, there remain major challenges that make the efficiency of vFHE impractical. Among these barriers, one of the most severe challenges is the algebraic gap between cyclotomic rings, which are mainly used in FHE encryption, and finite fields, which are mainly used by SNARGs. In this paper, we rely on a novel solution called ring switching to bridge this gap, making significant progress toward efficient vFHE. To highlight the efficiency of ring switching, we provide an open-source implementation of its application to homomorphic matrix-vector multiplication. Experimental results demonstrate high efficiency.
