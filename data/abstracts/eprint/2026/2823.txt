We revisit the security of OleF, t-OleF, and OlÆF and identify two independent security flaws. First, we establish a mask-compensation relation between continuation and termination processing in OleF and t-OleF. Exploiting this relation, we construct a two-encryption-query distinguisher against OleF with advantage $1 − 2^{−2n}$ . We further give two concrete AXU hash families for which the same attack applies to t-OleF, revealing flaws in the original DOSPRP and SPRTOP security proofs. We extend these attacks to OlÆF through valid encodings, breaking its privacy and integrity. In particular, the integrity forgery succeeds with probability 1 under full-domain verification when the target expansion length is 1 and extends to larger expansion lengths under suffix-only verification. Second, we refute the claimed ideal RUP integrity bound for variable-expansion online authenticated encryption. Even with an ideal tweakable online permutation, prior unverified decryption queries at smaller expansion lengths can reveal part of the target authentication suffix. We construct counterexamples showing that a single unverified decryption query or a single non-target verification query, followed by one forgery attempt, achieves a success probability of $3/8$, exceeding the claimed bound of $1/4$. We also derive generalized expressions for the forgery success probabilities of both attacks. Finally, we discuss the corresponding repair requirements.
