We revisit the use of out-of-domain samples in proximity gaps, introduced in DEEP-FRI [BGKS20]. With multiple samples, we obtain proximity gaps up to the list decoding radius of any linear code. We highlight two regimes that our results make possible, that were previously not possible. First, for general linear codes, our approach extends the standard proximity-gap guarantee from the one-and-a-half Johnson bound to the full Johnson bound, at the expense of a constant number of out-of-domain samples. Moreover, with a constant fraction of agreement loss, we can achieve error $O(1/q)$ when the field size $q$ is at least the blocklength $n$, adding $20-30$ bits of security in typical hash-based SNARK constructions using general linear codes Second, for Reed--Solomon codes, our bounds apply beyond the Johnson radius even when the list size, $L$ is very large, e.g. $L \gg 2^\lambda$. While standard proximity gaps would typically achieve trivial error probability in this setting, our approach shows that one can still obtain $2^{-\lambda}$ error probability using $O(\log L/\lambda)$ out-of-domain samples.
