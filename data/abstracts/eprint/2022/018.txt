In an accountable subgroup multi-signature (ASM), any subgroup $S$ of a group of signers can sign a message so that each member of $S$ is accountable for the signature. We propose three pairing-based ASM schemes, unforgeable under the co-CDH assumption in the random oracle and algebraic group models, even when the adversary corrupts all but one member and chooses their keys. The first, vASM, sets up the group with Feldman's verifiable secret sharing and a proof of knowledge of the sharing coefficients; its signature is one $G_1$ element. Without this proof, a rogue-key attack forges for any target subgroup of size at least two, even with proofs of possession when the group has at least three members. In the second, ASMwSA, the subgroup is fixed before signing; in the third, ASMwCA, a designated combiner authenticates it. In both, keys carry a proof of knowledge, without which a subset-sum attack becomes feasible for large groups. All our schemes verify with one pairing fewer than the ASM scheme of Boneh, Drijvers and Neven (BDN), at the cost of a costlier one-time setup. vASM verifies in 1.3 to 1.6 ms for up to 256 members, faster than BDN for up to 64 members even when its verifier stores per-group hashes. Like BDN, vASM and ASMwCA do not need $S$ before signing; in ASMwSA the combiner can be eliminated. The aggregated versions output one $G_1$ element for $N$ signatures and verify with $N+1$ pairings, against $N+1$ group elements and $2N+1$ pairings for BDN.
