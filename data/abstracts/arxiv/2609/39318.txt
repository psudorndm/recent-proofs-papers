We study two-prover one-round quantum interactive proofs in which the provers share arbitrarily many copies of a noisy EPR pair. Prior work showed that if the shared state $ψ$ has quantum maximal correlation $ρ_{\max}(ψ) < 1$, then $\mathrm{MIP}^ψ[\mathrm{poly}, O(1)] = \mathrm{MIP} = \mathrm{NEXP}$, while noiseless EPR pairs give $\mathrm{MIP}^{\mathrm{EPR}}[\mathrm{poly}, O(1)] = \mathrm{RE}$. That result leaves two cases open: answers of polynomial length, and noise that keeps $ρ_{\max}(ψ) = 1$, which unital noise can do. We settle both for unital noise. Equivalently, let $ψ$ be any two-qubit state with maximally mixed marginals. We show that $$\mathsf{MIP}^ψ[\mathrm{poly},\mathrm{poly}] =\mathsf{MIP} =\mathsf{NEXP},~\text{if $ψ$ is separable or $ρ_{\max}(ψ)<1$};$$ $$\mathsf{MIP}^ψ[\mathrm{poly},\mathrm{poly}] =\mathsf{RE}, \text{if $ψ$ is entangled and $ρ_{\max}(ψ)=1$.}$$ Beyond this complexity-theoretic dichotomy, our proof introduces two techniques of potential independent interest. The first technique approximates quantum measurements by positive low-degree operators. We prove that the square roots of the measurement elements, after applying the smoothing operation, can be approximated by low-degree operators, with error controlled in the Schatten 4-norm. This further gives a positive low-degree approximation with small normalization error. Second, we introduce Pauli folding, a randomized dimension-reduction method for low-degree quantum operators. For fixed accuracy, the number of qubits after folding is polynomial in the Pauli degree and the number of outcomes and does not depend on $n$. Moreover, Pauli folding preserves Hilbert-Schmidt inner products in expectation and is approximately multiplicative. This is an exponential improvement over the dimension reductions via invariance principles in all previous works.
