Incrementally verifiable computation (IVC) allows a prover to maintain a short proof that can be efficiently updated after each computation step, without retaining or recomputing the preceding execution. Standard IVC schemes do not directly address a crucial feature of AI tasks: their dependence on external information and expertise. We construct IVC schemes for oracle-aided computations under the LWE assumption by first constructing SNARGs, and then extending them to IVC. We consider the following two settings, where below, k is the security parameter, n is the input length, and T, S, and Q are the time, space, and oracle-query bounds. 1. Robust computations: For bounded-space computations, including non-deterministic computations, in which every accepting purported execution on a false input contains at least an eps-fraction of incorrect oracle answers, we construct privately verifiable SNARGs and IVC. Public parameters, private verification keys, and proofs have length poly(k ,S, log T,1/eps), and verification uses O(k/eps) oracle calls. We complement this result with a lower bound ruling out (deterministic) publicly verifiable SNARGs and IVCs with public parameters of length o(n), and o(Q) verifier oracle queries. 2. Signed oracles: For oracles that digitally sign their query-answer pairs, we construct publicly verifiable SNARGs and IVC without a robustness assumption, and with oracle-free setup and verification. Public parameters and proofs have length poly(k,S,log T). For deterministic computations with oracle query and answer lengths poly(k,log T), we remove the dependence on S, obtaining public parameter and proof lengths poly(k,log T). All our constructions have adaptive completeness and soundness.
