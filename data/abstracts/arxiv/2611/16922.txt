An important challenge in homomorphic encryption (HE) is ensuring the integrity of the encrypted computations. Although verifiable computation can address this in theory, making it practical is far from trivial. [Cascudo et al. CRYPTO 2025] recently proposed a solution to this problem for the CKKS scheme. In particular, they propose new succinct arguments that efficiently prove arithmetic circuits and range checks over the ring $R_q = \mathbb{Z}_q[Y]/(Y^N+1)$, and combine them to prove computations on CKKS ciphertexts. Despite the important progress, their work faces scalability challenges: the size of the polynomial commitment proofs, a large $O(N)$ overhead in range proofs, and the lack of an explicit method for efficient -- sublinear in circuit size -- verification. In this paper we present new techniques that remove all these bottlenecks. First, we provide a more efficient arithmetization of CKKS computations. Second, we introduce BatchFold, a new polynomial commitment for $R_q$ that supports batched evaluations and achieves shorter proofs. Third, we design packing, a novel algebraic technique that eliminates the need to commit to massive polynomials and removes the $O(N)$ overhead in range proofs. Finally, we introduce SparsePack, an efficient protocol for proving the evaluations of sparse, circuit-dependent polynomials, leveraging both packing and batched evaluations. We evaluate the efficiency of our solution on a generic neural network architecture, achieving proofs several orders of magnitude smaller and faster than prior approaches.
