We present QUASI, a newly conceived class of protocols that enable a group of parties to produce a publicly verifiable attestation about a pre-validated statement. Designed for quorum-based systems, QUASI enables parties to attest to external entities that the statement is valid. Surprisingly, each party need not sign the entire statement: authenticating only a small subset of symbols suffices for the quorum to attest to the whole. A simple deterministic construction illustrates this possibility. Built purely from hashing and error correction, QUASI achieves small per-party contributions and low attestation generation and verification costs, without relying on SNARKs for aggregation. Our main contribution is a randomized construction achieving reductions of more than $10\times$ in per-party contribution size and hash computations for generation and verification, compared with full hash-based signatures. QUASI is a generic primitive supporting diverse applications. Two principal use cases are external attestation for signature-free consensus, enabling clients to verify statements about committed state; and preprocessing for SNARK-based aggregation, compounding QUASI's savings with succinct-proof compression.
