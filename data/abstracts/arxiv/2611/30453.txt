Permissioned-ledger cross-domain data sharing needs anonymity and accountability: an endorsement hides its author, but a regulator must identify it on authorization reuse. Existing lattice-based schemes give these only separately: public traceability exposes signers to collision detectors; linkable non-tracing schemes cannot open; threshold schemes solve another problem. None gives post-quantum security, a signer-chosen ring, a publicly comparable per-event tag, and one-authority opening together. We propose \textsf{PLATRS}, a module-lattice linkable accountable ring signature for permissioned blockchains. Consortium-CA enrollment binds the key returned by an opening to a registered identity. The signer chooses the ring; verification is index-free; each signature has a per-event tag any verifier can compare. Tag noise derives from the signer's tracing secret and event, so linking is one comparison for honest signatures while the tag remains M-LWE without the secret. Detection is two-tier: tag mismatch is immediate; different tags on two accepted signatures for one event trigger an opening; the authority publishes the recovered public opening component, and any member recomputes the verdict against the ring. We prove CPA anonymity from M-LWE, linkability, authority-only traceability, and unforgeability from M-SIS with M-LWE in the random oracle model. At \(N=1024\), our open-source implementation: sign 9.886~ms, verify 3.919~ms, link 0.7~\(μ\)s, open 480~\(μ\)s; signature 107.3~KiB (log in ring size); adjudication 4.867~ms. Hyperledger Fabric: 60.5 signature submissions/s at 7.87~MB/s with 16~MiB blocks, commit-bandwidth limited. Common-harness: fastest of 5 schemes to sign/verify, second in size; vs FLLL\(^+\)21, signing is 3188\(\times\) faster and signatures 133\(\times\) shorter.
