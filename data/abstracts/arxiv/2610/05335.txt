Proofs of authenticated-data-structure updates built on the Goldwasser-Kalai-Rothblum (GKR) protocol are sound only if three arguments hold together: the soundness of the protocol, the exactness of the arithmetization that turns an update into a circuit, and the binding of the hash commitments. For GKR, the mechanizations we know of stop at the sumcheck subprotocol. For sparse-Merkle membership, non-membership and update as compiled by the StateSync-GKR prover, we machine-check in Isabelle/HOL the first two arguments, their composition, and the leaf side of the third. For layered circuits with linear, product and cube gates, the cube gate serving the $x^3$ S-box of Poseidon2, we prove that for a false output claim, the event that some layer's sumcheck accepts a false sum while all earlier carried claims are false has probability at most $(s_{\mathrm{out}} + \sum_i (2 s_i d_i + 1))/|\mathbb{F}|$, where layer $i$ has $2^{s_i}$ wires and $d_i$ bounds the degree of its sumcheck polynomial. We instantiate the bound over the exact quartic extension of the 31-bit KoalaBear field from which small-field provers draw challenges, so that the denominator is $p^4 \approx 2^{124}$, and lift base-field layers and false outputs into it. In a constraint-level model of the compiler, with the hash sub-circuits as abstract functions, the verifier model accepts exactly the valid transitions, and leaf-commitment collisions reduce to sponge collisions. Their composition is mechanized for claims that no witness makes valid, and from the mechanized theorems it follows on paper that the bad event has probability at most this bound for every input that does not encode a valid transition. All bounds concern the interactive public-coin protocol with uniform challenges; the Fiat-Shamir transformation, knowledge soundness and zero knowledge are outside the model.
