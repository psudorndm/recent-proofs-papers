Local Coordinate-wise Linear (LCL) properties, introduced by Levi, Mosheiff, and Shagrithaya (2025), capture coding-theoretic properties such as list decoding and list recovery. They showed that every LCL property has the same rate threshold for random linear codes (RLC) and random Reed--Solomon codes (RRS). Brakensiek, Chen, Dhar, and Zhang (2025) extended this to random additive codes. Thus any property of all subspace design codes holds for RLCs and RRS at the same rate. In this work, we improve the probabilistic guarantees in the RRS local equivalence theorem and extend the equivalence to all known subspace design constructions individually. Each such construction, including folded Reed--Solomon codes, has the same LCL threshold as RLC and RRS codes. We apply these results to alphabet-size bounds for curve decoding. Curve decoding, introduced by Goyal and Guruswami (2025) and Carmon et al. (2026), is a row-span-constrained LCL property implying proximity gaps (PG), correlated agreement (CA), and mutual correlated agreement (MCA), relevant to IOPs and SNARKs. Informally, a rate-$R$ code $C \subset \mathbb{F}^n$ is $(\ell,δ,a,b)$-curve decodable if any degree-$\ell$ curve $u:\mathbb{F}\to\mathbb{F}^n$ that is $δ$-close to $C$ on at least $a$ points has at least $b\leq a$ nearby codewords lying on a degree-$\ell$ curve in $C$. Goyal and Guruswami (2025) and Goyal, Guruswami, Sun, and Wootters (2026) established curve decodability for RLC and RRS codes at radius $δ$ approaching $1-R$, but with large alphabets in $\ell$ and $η:=1-R-δ$. Using our improved LCL equivalence and rank-sensitive counting inspired by Goyal and Guruswami (2026), we exponentially improve alphabet requirements while matching the best known values of $(a,b)$. For $\ell=O(1)$, our alphabet-size bounds match the best known for list decoding, and improves results for PG, CA, and MCA.
